Briefing

Field manual

The cores

Raiders come for the power cores in your vault and haul them to a gate. Destroy a carrier and its cores drop, then float home on their own. Raiders passing a dropped core pick it up. Lose every core and the sector falls.

Hills

  • Peak gates sit on mountaintops; valley gates open at the end of canyons. Roads wind between them and your vault.
  • Towers on higher ground than the vault get up to +35% range.
  • Raiders slow down climbing and speed up going downhill.
  • Grey teal corner ticks mark buildable ground. Steep slopes, rock and lakes cannot hold towers.

Twin vaults and Riftbots

Some sectors split your cores between two vaults, A and B. Raiders go for the closer one; when a vault is empty they all head for the other. The HUD shows what each vault holds. Riftbots open a blue portal where they die and a yellow one further down the road for 5 seconds; raiders that reach the blue one jump to the yellow one.

Bridges, tunnels and ramps

Now and then a road crosses a valley on a bridge, so some raiders walk the canyon floor underneath while others cross over the top, all heading for the same vault. Tunnels bore through ridges: raiders inside are out of sight and cannot be hit, so cover both portals (the amber dots over the hill trace the route underneath). Terraced sectors stack flat levels with cliffs between them, joined by ramps with amber chevrons. Nothing can be built on tunnels, bridges or the ground under a bridge.

Routes

Raiders prefer roads but will cut across open ground. Towers and electric fences block tiles, so you can wall off shortcuts. Pick Build routes in sector select to also build on roads and design the whole maze. You can never seal a route completely.

Raiders that fight back

Gunners, Lancers, Mortars, Gunships and Behemoths shoot your towers. A tower at zero health goes offline, then recovers on its own after a few quiet seconds. Repair it instantly from its info panel. Wreckers walk up to a tower and plant a charge that destroys it outright.

Air and warps

Flyers come from the sky lanes on the map edge (blue beams and dotted lines) straight for the vault, then leave by the nearest edge. Phasers warp in a few tiles from the vault; they cannot warp back out with a core. A Warp Anchor keeps them from landing inside its field.

Know your enemy

The roster at the top shows a picture, count, health, speed and armor for every raider in the coming wave. Click one for its full card. The first time a raider appears you get its card automatically. Play Master Training to meet them all.

Controls

  • 1–9, 0 pick a build option, click to place, Esc or right-click to cancel
  • Drag or W A S D to pan, wheel or +/- to zoom (the wheel zooms toward the cursor)
  • Right-drag (or Ctrl+drag) to spin and tilt, Q/E rotate, Page Up/Page Down tilt, O auto-orbit, Home reset
  • U upgrade, R repair, Del sell, T cycle targeting
  • Space next wave, F game speed, P pause
  • Touch: tap a tile to preview, tap again to build. Pinch to zoom, twist to rotate.

Build mode

Endless waves in series of ten, a bit tougher every series, with a Behemoth at the end of each. Choose the field size, terrain, gates and biome; pick Open field to get no roads at all and design the entire course with towers and fences. Your best run per field size is saved.

Saving and stars

Three save slots keep unlocked sectors, stars and one battle in progress. Three stars for holding every core, two for keeping at least half, one for surviving.
